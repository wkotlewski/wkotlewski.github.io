SkyGround

A phone-first sky explorer I designed and built for curious students. Point your phone at the sky to find satellites, planets, the Moon, and bright stars, then tap one to learn what it does, how high and fast it orbits, and what place on Earth is below it right now. Orbits, ground tracks, and place stories connect space to the ground, and everything is calculated on the device from real orbital data, so a student's location never leaves their phone.

Star Knight League

An adaptive learning game I designed and built for my 3rd grader. Kids bat, pitch, and catch in a space baseball league, dodge, shoot, and save in hover-soccer, shoot hoops in zero gravity, battle a space fleet, and follow a rescue trail. Math, spelling, vocabulary, and reading challenges aligned to California Common Core power up each play, and wins earn real-life prizes. Missed skills come back more often, and a grown-ups view tracks accuracy by skill. It runs in any phone, tablet, or desktop browser and works offline.

Dress-Up Learning Games

A tap-to-play dress-up game I designed and built for my 4-year-old. She dresses a character in themed outfits, paints nails, and picks hairstyles, while the game says every color, shape, number, and letter out loud. Learning questions pop up as she plays, and she can tap an answer or say it out loud. Hints appear after two misses, and a grown-ups menu sets how often questions show up. It runs in any phone, tablet, or desktop browser.

Songbird

A pocket music studio I designed and built for anyone with a hum or a song in their heart. Hum, sing, clap, or play a few bars, and it loops on the beat like a loop pedal so you can stack layers into a full band. Plain-language Magic edits turn requests like “add a horn section” or “make it tight” into changes, with genre and era sounds from the 1920s to today. Every change can be undone, and the finished song downloads from any phone, tablet, or desktop browser.

Simple Forever Plan

A phone-first app I designed and built for telling your life story and leaving your family what they’ll need, in your own words and voice. A gentle interview guides you chapter by chapter, and you answer by talking, recording video, or writing. Relatives can add what they remember with a single text. Answers become a written book, audiobook, or chapter playlist in the storyteller’s own voice, alongside a medical binder, family tree, and words to keep. Everything stays on the phone unless the storyteller chooses to share.
